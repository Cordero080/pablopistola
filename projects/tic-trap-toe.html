TIC-TRAP-TOE

Six faces. One rotating cube. First to win 3 boards wins the match — the rotation is the mechanic, not the decoration.

GitHub

Desktop Only — Requires mouse input for raycasting click detection on the 3D cube faces.

The Concept

A standard tic-tac-toe board has one game. This has six — one on each face of a cube that never stops rotating. As faces rotate toward the camera their boards become available to play. As they rotate away, you lose access. There is no timer and no forced turn order between faces. If you can see the cells, you can play them.

Each face maintains its own independent game state — its own board, its own X/O turn, its own win condition. At any moment you may be mid-game on two or three faces simultaneously, each at a different tempo. Knowing when to commit to a face versus letting it rotate away is the core skill.

How the Rotation Works

Three.js raycasting handles click detection natively — FrontSide backface culling means you physically cannot click a cell that is rotating away from you. No artificial threshold needed. The physics enforce the rules.

Won Face Visuals

When a player wins a face: confetti bursts from the exact winning cell in world space (44 neon particles scatter along the face normal, arc under gravity, fade out). The face surface shifts to a metallic white holographic overlay that color-cycles continuously. The three winning marks turn red with emissive glow; the losing marks go near-black. The score digit scrambles before landing.

On match win, the cube decelerates exponentially and fires a damped scale pulse — two sharp thumps, then stillness.

Key Features

World-Space Confetti
